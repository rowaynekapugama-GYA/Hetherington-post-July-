David Salim

NSW Minerals Council's Exploration & Tenures Forum

Main sponsor — 10th consecutive year 4 May · InterContinental Sydney

Hetherington was proud to return as the main sponsor of the NSW Minerals Council's Exploration & Tenures Forum 2026 for the tenth consecutive year. David Salim had the privilege of opening the day with the welcome address before a packed program of case studies and expert insights got underway.

Assistant Manager Jemima Moffat took to the stage in the first session to share her insights on land access case studies and tips for success, and David was joined by Karinne, Emily, Olivia, Yung and Vivian for the day, which also covered carbon farming, exploration and mine approvals, and closed with government updates from NSW Resources and the NSW Resources Regulator.

The forum concluded with a guest address by the Hon Courtney Houssos MLC — a fitting close to a day that brought together chief executives, exploration managers, titles managers and consultants from across the sector. It was a strong way to start the week, surrounded by so many of the conversations that matter most to our industry.

Cultural heritage and native title in focus at AMEC

Sponsor Perth, WA

Hetherington was proud to sponsor AMEC's Cultural Heritage to Native Title Forum in Perth, where David Salim was honoured to give the opening address alongside government, industry and legal leaders, exploring where Cultural Heritage and Native Title are heading in WA post the Kelly Review. Our own Madelaine Cannell-Lunn also contributed to the conversation.

We were also pleased to host an AMEC Boardroom Lunch at the Melbourne Hotel in Perth, joined by Hon Matthew Swinbourn MLC and fellow AMEC members.

These forums matter more than most people realise. Cultural heritage and native title sit at the centre of nearly every project decision in this industry, and the landscape continues to shift. Thank you to AMEC, the speakers, the panellists and everyone who came along.

NTNorthern Territory

Mineral titles framework modernised: the Amendment Act is now in force

The Northern Territory has modernised its mineral titles framework with the Mineral Titles Legislation Amendment Act 2026 (NT). Introduced in March 2026 and in force from 5 June, it brings changes to exploration licences, new title categories for small-scale and fossicking activities, and updates to preliminary exploration requirements.

Read the full update

On 18 March 2026, the Northern Territory Minister for Mining and Energy, Hon Gerald Maley MLA, introduced the Mineral Titles Legislation Amendment Bill 2026 (NT). The bill was passed on 14 May 2026, assented to on 4 June 2026 and commenced on 5 June 2026, now being the Mineral Titles Legislation Amendment Act 2026 (NT) ("Amendment Act").

Key takeaways

The Amendment Act amends the Mineral Titles Act 2010 (NT), the Mineral Titles Regulations 2011 (NT), the Environment Protection Act 2019 (NT) and the Environment Protection Regulations 2020 (NT), addressing minor issues identified since the introduction of the Environmental (Mining) Licensing Scheme. It aims to improve the efficiency and transparency of exploration and mining processes by simplifying application pathways, modernising reporting requirements, and introducing more flexible, proportionate approaches to renewals, reductions and reporting.

New title categories

New mineral title categories are being introduced to better reflect the diversity of mining and fossicking activities: Mineral Leases for Small-Scale Mines, Tourist Fossicking and Fossicking — fit-for-purpose arrangements for activities not captured under the existing legislation, reducing administrative burden on small operators and fossicking businesses.

  • The Mineral Lease for Small-Scale Mines supports operations such as alluvial gold mining, with reporting requirements reduced or simplified when activity and risk are low, and less stringent application requirements — excluding the requirement to provide evidence of an economic resource.
  • It also supports the transition of non-compliant existing interests, preserving the holder's rights (subject to compliance with title conditions such as annual rent, administration fees, an expiry date and a right of renewal) while aligning them with the contemporary legislative framework.
  • Tourist Fossicking and Fossicking titles support individuals and tourist operations over relatively small areas, allowing mechanical means to push back topsoil to a maximum depth of one metre. A fossicking permit will be available to individuals, family groups, clubs and commercial tour groups for a fee, with a reduced-fee NT resident permit.

Exploration licences

  • Renewal period extended from two to six years.
  • Biennial title reduction requirements removed — reduction now required at first renewal.
  • A summary of the proposed technical work program for the following four years will be required.
  • Minimum size of an exploration licence reduced from four blocks to one.
  • Renewals must be received one month prior to the title's expiry date, be accompanied by the rent for the operational year, and reduce the title area by 50%.

Extractive mineral exploration licences

Improvements for extractive minerals operators include longer grant periods, a 30-day moratorium over land previously subject to a ceased extractive mineral exploration licence (allowing for more competitive applications), and the ability to move extractive materials from one extractive mineral permit to another for processing, storage and removal.

Preliminary exploration

The Amendment Act clarifies preliminary exploration requirements, particularly on Aboriginal land, ensuring consent processes align with the Aboriginal Land Act 1978 (NT) rather than the more onerous section 41 processes under the Aboriginal Land Rights (Northern Territory) Act 1976 (Cth). Airborne geoscientific surveys no longer require the Minister's approval; instead, proponents must give the Minister at least 30 days' notice, and the Minister may impose conditions, including written notification to affected stakeholders.

Key impacts

The Amendment Act represents a significant modernisation of the Northern Territory's mineral titles system — strengthening environmental safeguards, clarifying land access and consent processes, and delivering a more accessible and efficient legislative framework.
